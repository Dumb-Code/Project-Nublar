## Key question 1: For each creature mod, what feeding, taming, growth, breeding, aggression, riding/command, machine, feeder, enrichment, weapon and vehicle rules are documented, with numbers?

### Takeaway
Fossils and Archeology Revival (F/A) is the only reference mod whose wiki documents a complete fossil-to-dinosaur machine chain with numbers (15% DNA per bio-fossil, 10% culture-vat failure, 100 s egg hatch, 6-block imprint radius, 6-block feeder detection, food-value tables, a -100..100 mood scale, 12-day growth for large species, 5-minute breeding). Alex's Caves documents the most vanilla-feeling modern dinosaur rules (32-block aggro, 6-block tempt-follow, 8-10 day eggs halved on Fern Thatch, 10-block imprint, 1-day baby growth, 5-minute breed cooldown, Elder-led packs of up to 8 raptors). Ice and Fire documents a 5-stage, 125-day growth model with stage-gated riding and a three-command staff (stay / wander-at-home / escort). Nothing in these wikis documents a dinosaur-park vehicle, electric fence, or firearm; the closest analogues are F/A's whip-riding and javelin, and Ice and Fire's dragon armor and flight controls.

### Cited Findings

#### Fossils and Archeology Revival (official wiki fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg; Modrinth)
- Versions and chain summary: the wiki main page states the newest public version is 9.3.2.0 for 1.18.2-1.20.1 on Forge and Fabric, depending on Architectury API, More Hitboxes, GeckoLib and TerraBlender [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Fossils_and_Archeology_Wiki). Modrinth lists 1.20.1, 1.19.2-1.19.4, 1.18.2, 1.12.2 and 1.7.10 on Fabric and Forge, "60+" creatures, and points to fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg and GitHub TeamFossilsArcheology/FossilsArcheologyRevival [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/fossils-and-archeology-revival). Maps to B00 (context). VF: neutral.
- Fossil block (B01-B03): veins "between coal and iron" size; iron pickaxe or better; Fortune applies; seven host-stone variants (stone, red sandstone, sandstone, tuff, deepslate, calcite, dripstone); tuff and deepslate variants drop Shale Fossil, the rest Bio-Fossil; documented drop table: Bio-Fossil/Shale 22.5%, Relics 23%, Bones 40%, Skull 10%, Broken Ancient Sword 5%, Broken Ancient Helmet 5%, Scarab Gem 0.1%, plus cobblestone; the Paleontology and Archaeology enchantments raise the bio-fossil and relic chances [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Fossil). VF: ok (ore-like block with a loot table).
- Sifter (B01-B03/B05): accepts sand, dirt, gravel, red sand, podzol, soul sand and modded sand/dirt; the block is consumed and yields one of bio-fossil, sand, carrot, potato, fossilized fern spores, bone meal, pottery shard, Dominican amber, plant fossil, or nothing [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Sifter). Dominican amber is a 0.2% sifter drop and is the only normal amber source [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Dominican_Amber). VF: ok.
- Permafrost (B01-B03, B21): generates in the dirt layer of snowy plains; melts to dirt under strong torch light or sunlight; Silk Touch needed for the block; drops fern seeds, frozen meat, bone, book, skull; Expert Paleontologist villagers sell 4 permafrost for 2 emeralds [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Permafrost). VF: ok.
- Analyzer (B05/B06): crafted from iron ingots plus one bio-fossil and one relic scrap; inputs and outputs: bio-fossil -> dinosaur DNA; tar fossil or frozen meat -> mammal/bird DNA; plant fossil -> fossilized seeds; relic scrap -> stone tablet, pottery shard, figurine or broken sword/helmet; any mod animal meat -> that species' DNA at 100%; farm-animal meat -> that animal's DNA at 100%; leather -> cow and horse DNA; feather -> chicken; wool -> sheep; junk outputs are sand, bone meal, gravel, flint, meat, coal, cactus green, string; cooked meat and vanilla bones cannot be analyzed [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Analyzer). VF: ok as a furnace-like block; the 100% meat-to-DNA path is a documented shortcut around fossil RNG.
- DNA odds (B05/B06): bio-fossil 15% Mesozoic (orange) DNA; shale fossil 15% Paleozoic (green); frozen meat 20% Cenozoic (blue); tar fossil 20% Cenozoic; animal meat 100%; first DNA grants the "Bingo! Dino DNA!" advancement [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/DNA). VF: neutral.
- Culture Vat (B11/B12/B13): recipe 4 glass, 3 iron ingots, 1 slimeball, 1 water bucket; DNA or plant fossil in the top slot, Bio-Goo in the bottom slot; takes "a few minutes"; 10% failure chance (stated as configurable) which destroys the vat, drops contents and spawns a Failuresaurus or rarely a creeper, zombie horse, mooshroom or zombie pigman; plant failures make a failure plant [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Culture_Vat). Bio-Goo recipe: rotten flesh + milk + wheat + egg (Failuresaurus flesh substitute gives 4); one Bio-Goo covers one full cultivation and is consumed at start [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Bio-Goo). VF: conflict (a machine that can randomly explode into a hostile mob is not vanilla-like; the 10% failure is the documented "risk" lever).
- Embryo (B11 analogue): mammal DNA yields an embryo; inject into pig, cow, sheep, goat, wolf, cat or horse (quagga only horse); birth after about 10000 ticks (8 min 20 s) by default; the analyzer reverts an embryo to DNA [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Embryo). VF: neutral.
- Non-avian eggs (B11, B18): placeable egg models, picked up by right-click; hatching takes 100 seconds; progress stops if conditions are bad or the egg is picked up; the egg needs enough space for the baby, otherwise the message "This Egg Cannot Spawn Here" appears after the timer; terrestrial and amphibious eggs must be on land in a warm area (sun or torch light); cold or dry eggs do not progress and eventually despawn ("die"); aquatic sacs hatch in water; avian eggs are thrown; breeding needs a male and a female (except quagga); a config option makes bred eggs drop as items like chicken eggs to reduce lag and chat spam [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Non-Avian_Eggs). VF: ok (turtle-egg-like).
- Taming rules (B19): standing within six blocks of the egg or pregnant mammal imprints the newborn; the seven apex species (Tyrannosaurus, Allosaurus, Spinosaurus, Sarcosuchus, Megalania, Megalodon, Mosasaurus) are the exception: they must be adult, be fought down to 8 HP, lie down "asleep", and be right-clicked with a Scarab Gem (Aquatic Scarab Gem for aquatic species) [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Prehistoric_Creatures). The whip tames almost everything else by repeated right-click; each hit lowers mood but raises tame chance; works at all growth stages [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Whip). Scarab gems drop from fossil blocks at 0.01% (1 in 10,000) per that page, and rarely in dungeon chests; aquatic scarab = scarab + Dominican amber [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Scarab_Gem). Note: the Fossil page says 0.1% for the same drop [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Fossil); the two pages conflict. VF: conflict (fight-to-near-death taming is not a vanilla pattern; imprint-on-hatch is close to vanilla cat/wolf trust).
- Commands (B19): tamed creatures take sit, follow and free-move orders; they always defend their owner regardless of order; hungry creatures ignore orders to seek food and the player receives chat messages when they are hungry; command items: stick for herbivores, bone for carnivores, Skull Stick (skull + stick) for apex predators, empty shell for Plesiosaurus, arrows for Pteranodon; quagga cannot be commanded [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Prehistoric_Creatures), [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Skull_Stick). VF: conflict on chat spam (vanilla never messages the player about pets); the three-state order set matches vanilla wolf sit/follow.
- Riding (B22 analogue): rideable list is Allosaurus, Brachiosaurus, Dilophosaurus, Diplodocus, Ceratosaurus, Elasmotherium, Gallimimus, Mammoth, Megalania, Megaloceros, Megalodon, Mosasaurus, Ornitholestes, Triceratops, Tyrannosaurus; mount when the Dinopedia says it is old enough; WASD control only while holding a whip; without the whip the mount wanders; terrestrial mounts are slow and hard to control in water, Spinosaurus and Sarcosuchus are faster underwater; aquatic mounts beach on land; aquatic riding uses look up/down to ascend/descend [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Prehistoric_Creatures), [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Whip). VF: conflict (vanilla uses a saddle, not a held item, for steering).
- Care and containment (B15/B16/B17): creatures must be fed, protected from predators and given room to grow; Chicken Essence ages a creature by one day at the cost of hunger (documented drain "under 10" to "over 100"), not usable on apex predators, 8 per cooked chicken soup + 8 bottles [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Chicken_Essence); low hunger triggers a message and then starvation death; low mood makes even tamed creatures hostile and stops reproduction; some creatures break any block weaker than iron, so Reinforced Glass or Dense Sand are the documented safe walls [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Prehistoric_Creatures). Reinforced Glass "has the same strength value of iron" and no dinosaur can break it; made by smelting Dense Sand (creative-only in 1.12.2) [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Reinforced_Glass). VF: conflict (block breaking by livestock is the single most vanilla-breaking rule in F/A; it exists precisely to force iron-tier containment).
- Feeder block (B20, B17): stores food for tamable dinosaurs; dinosaurs detect feeders within a 6-block radius; two internal pools, carnivore and herbivore; the feeder must be within 2 blocks of the dinosaur's head to feed; Velociraptors use feeders only when no prey is available; Triceratops check feeders before ferns; documented carnivore values include raw porkchop 35, cooked porkchop 55, raw fish 15-20, raw beef 40, steak 60, raw chicken 10, cooked chicken 15, chicken egg 7, dino egg 7, most raw mod meat 35 (herbivore species) or 40 (carnivore species), most cooked mod meat 60, Failuresaurus flesh 15; herbivore values include apple 20, wheat 13, bread 25, melon block 65, melon slice 10, mushroom 15, oak leaves 20, Palaeoraphe leaves 40, carrot 15, potato 35, cake 50, sugar 7, sugar cane 15, poppy/dandelion 5, pumpkin 30, pumpkin pie 25, any seeds 5, saplings 15; recipe uses iron, glass, bucket, stone button and stone [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Feeder). VF: neutral (new block, but hopper-like).
- Mood / stress (B17, B19): mood is -100..100; Content (50-99) and Happy (100): attacks only if attacked; Calm (-49..49): herbivores attack only when provoked, carnivores only when hungry, except the "very aggressive" Spinosaurus, Sarcosuchus, Ceratosaurus, Liopleurodon, Mosasaurus and Tyrannosaurus; Sad (-99..-50) and Angry (-100): attack everything on sight; mood rises from playing with enrichment, killing an entity, eating a dropped item, or being hand-fed; mood falls from damage dealt by another entity, from exceeding the species' "max population" nearby, and from incompatible species nearby [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Mood), [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Happiness_Scale). The Dinopedia (bio-fossil + book) shows age, health, hunger, diet, temperament, gender, taming status, current order, command item, activity style, mood bar and accepted foods on right-click [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Dinopedia). VF: conflict (a visible numeric mood is non-vanilla; the behavioural consequences are reasonable).
- Enrichment items (B17/B20): Scratching Post (5 wool + stick + wooden slab) is an entity with 10 hearts, placed on the ground, meant to be indestructible but one-hit killers break it; Tethered Log (2 string + log) hangs under a block, has half a heart and is often broken; unhappy creatures "play" by running their attack animation at the item [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Scratching_Post), [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Tethered_Log). VF: neutral.
- Tyrannosaurus numbers (B16/B17/B18/B19): adult 82 HP, newborn 15 HP, damage 16, hunger 150; about 4 blocks tall, 12 long, 3 wide; newborn half a block; fully grown in 12 Minecraft days; cannot take Chicken Essence; adults need high mood to not attack on sight; breaks blocks weaker than iron; when hostile it is faster than a sprinting player with Speed II; most of the hitbox is "arrowproof"; diurnal, sleeps at night; adults breed and lay eggs every five minutes if a male and female are present, with probability scaled by how many individuals are nearby; taming at 8 HP (4 hearts) with scarab, adult = at least 12 days old [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Tyrannosaurus). VF: conflict (arrow-proof hitbox and Speed-II pursuit are anti-vanilla by design).
- Velociraptor numbers (B16/B17/B18/B15): adult 22 HP, infant 4 HP, damage 4, hunger 75; 0.6 blocks tall, 2 long; grows in six Minecraft days; feathers on by default (config); leap attack then latches and bites every second for 2 hearts; the only escape is water at least three blocks deep; climbs walls like spiders so enclosures need a roof; raptors do not defend each other and rarely attack each other; breed every 5 minutes; tamed by being within 6 blocks of the egg, by hand-feeding, or by whip; commanded with a bone; hunts rather than using feeders; eats only meat [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Velociraptor). VF: conflict on wall-climbing livestock; neutral otherwise.
- Triceratops numbers (B18/B19/B22): about 3 blocks tall at the shoulder (4 at the frill), 8.5 long, 3 wide; newborn half a block; 12 Minecraft days to grow; herd animals that defend each other, including babies; territorial but gentle if left alone; breaks blocks weaker than iron; will not enter water to attack; diurnal; breed/lay eggs every five minutes; tamed by the 6-block egg rule, hand-feeding or whip; stick commands; rideable when tamed and fully grown via whip [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Triceratops). VF: herd defence is vanilla-like (wolves, zombified piglins).
- Javelin (B23 analogue): uncraftable ranged weapon obtained from Archaeologist villagers or Hell Boat chests; works like a bow (hold to charge); damage wood 1 heart, stone 1.5, iron 2, gold 1, diamond 2.5, ancient 3.5 hearts; durability 5 (ancient 3); ricochets and embeds in the ground for pickup; can be fired while riding without hurting the mount and from dispensers; repaired in the Archeology Workbench with relic scraps; cannot be enchanted [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Javelin). VF: ok (bow-like).
- Villagers and boss (B21): the mod adds an Archaeologist villager and hostile mobs around the boss Anu [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Mobs); the Archaeologist Villager page now redirects to an "(old)" page [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Archaeologist_Villager). VF: ok.

#### Ice and Fire (fandom wiki; CurseForge) and Ice and Fire: Community Edition (Modrinth; docs.iafenvoy.com)
- Growth (B16): dragons have five stages; each stage takes 25 Minecraft days, 125 days to maximum; Dragon Meal ages one day per meal; stage 3 unlocks riding with breath and bite attacks; attacks scale with stage [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Dragons). Fire Dragon HP by stage: 1 = 20-112, 2 = 116-208, 3 = 212-304, 4 = 308-400, 5 = 404-500; attack 35; length grows from 2 blocks at 0 days to 50 blocks fully grown; growth table days 0-24 / 25-49 / 50-74 / 75-99 / 100-124 / 125; Sickly Dragon Meal stops growth; male wing spots appear on day 10 of stage 1; fully grown dragons keep ageing without growing [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Fire_Dragon). The CE docs repeat the HP table and add that stage 4-5 dragons spawn only in underground caves, fire dragons in biomes with temperature above -0.5, ice at or below -0.5, lightning in jungle/savanna/badlands [doc](https://docs.iafenvoy.com/docs/mod/ice-and-fire-ce/mobs/dragons). VF: conflict (125 days is far beyond vanilla's 20-minute baby growth; a smooth-growth model with stage gates is the reusable idea).
- Eggs and hatching (B11): eggs drop only from female stage 4-5 dragons (stage 5 up to 3); fire eggs must be placed in fire, ice eggs in water that freezes, lightning eggs exposed to rain; rain stopping pauses hatching; default hatch time is 7200 ticks (6 minutes), configurable; the hatchling is tamed if the player is near; punching the egg returns it [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_Egg). Breeding needs opposite genders at stage 3 or higher and Fire Lily Mixture; the female builds a nest and lays eggs [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Fire_Dragon). VF: neutral (element-specific incubation conditions are a documented "environment-gated incubator" pattern).
- Commands and control items (B19): Dragon Command Staff (dragon skull + 2 sticks) cycles Escorting (follows; from stage 2 flies circles over you), Staying, Wandering (free but never leaves its home position); home position set/cleared by shift-right-click [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_Command_Staff). Dragon Horn stores a tamed dragon as an item and re-summons it by holding right-click; dragons do not grow inside a horn [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_Horn). Stage 1 dragons ride on the shoulder (max 3), stage 2 cannot be picked up, stage 3+ are mounted by empty-hand interaction; controls: WASD, Space to rise (hold over a second on the ground to take off), X to descend, Shift to dismount, R to breathe, G for a strike at the looked-at mob; Dragon Bone Flute calls a flying dragon down; Dragon Summoning Crystal teleports a bound dragon; dragon inventory has 4 armor slots plus a banner slot [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Fire_Dragon). VF: conflict (keybound mount abilities are non-vanilla; the stay / wander-near-home / escort trio is a clean command set).
- Aggression and detection (B17/B19): wild fire dragons are hostile, spawn "very hungry" and hunt farm animals, can spot prey from 64 blocks, sleep at roosts at night and wake if a player breaks a gold pile or opens a nearby chest, rarely eat dropped meat; stage 4+ roars inflict Weakness for 14 s (earplugs nullify); dragons break blocks to reach targets (a "weak" setting cannot break anything harder than stone; the mod follows mobGriefing since 1.1.0) [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Fire_Dragon). Hippogryphs are neutral but attack players and farm animals when hungry, deal 5 HP bites, regenerate slowly, are tamed by dropping about 8 rabbit feet, sit when tamed, take saddle/chest/armor, toggle wander/sit with a stick, defend their owner, and accept a home position via sneak + stick or staff [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Hippogryph). VF: hunger-gated hostility is a documented middle ground between vanilla neutral and hostile mobs.
- Carcass (B16): a dead dragon falls as a corpse instead of vanishing; the player right-clicks repeatedly with an empty hand for drops until it becomes a skeleton and then disappears; a glass bottle takes blood instead of other loot; newly hatched dragons vanish on death, leave a skeleton after day 5 and a corpse only after day 10; a fully grown dragon yields about 46 bones and either 12 blood or 58 scales [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Fire_Dragon). VF: conflict (vanilla has no carcass stage), but this is the one documented reference for the B16 carcass feature.
- Other tamables as command/taming patterns (B19): Hippocampus is tamed by repeated kelp/sponge feeding, bred with prismarine crystals, grows up in 1 day, is ridden with WASD and look-steering, and grants water breathing to the rider [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Hippocampus); Amphithere is tamed by mounting it and surviving its bites, then follows / wanders / sits via sneak-interaction [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Amphithere); Cockatrice is tamed by surviving its stare, then follows, patrols a 50-block radius, wanders or sits; rotten-egg babies are auto-tamed, grow up in 20 minutes (1 Minecraft day), and the species is sterile [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Cockatrice). VF: patrol-radius is a documented fourth order state worth copying.
- Armor (post-Beta): 8 dragon armor materials equip into 4 slots via sneak-right-click; a full set needs 189 ingots [doc](https://ice-and-fire-mod.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_Armor).
- Original mod versions (KQ3 cross-ref): CurseForge lists 1.20.1, 1.19.2, 1.18.2, 1.17.1, 1.16.5 and older, Forge and NeoForge, with older builds needing Citadel or LLibrary [doc](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/ice-and-fire-dragons).
- Community Edition: Modrinth lists 1.21.1 and 1.20.1 on Fabric, Forge and NeoForge; it is an unofficial fork with optimizations, rewrites and new content, drops the Myrmex content, gives 1.21.1 new features and 1.20.1 bug fixes only, configures through a Jupiter-based in-game GUI that needs operator permission, moves biome settings to datapacks, and warns not to swap it into an existing original-mod save [doc](https://modrinth.com/project/VpmCsizY). The docs site lists config files `iaf-client.json` (visual) and `iaf-common.json` (server rules) under `config/iceandfire`, and says the Forge build is GitHub-only [doc](https://docs.iafenvoy.com/docs/mod/ice-and-fire-ce). VF: neutral.

#### Alex's Mobs (Modrinth) and Citadel (Modrinth, GitHub wiki)
- Alex's Mobs has no readable public wiki: alexs-mobs.fandom.com returns a "no English wiki at this URL" page and alexsmobs.wiki.gg returns 401 Not Authorized (onboarding) [doc](https://alexs-mobs.fandom.com/wiki/Taming), [doc](https://alexsmobs.wiki.gg/wiki/Elephant). The in-game Animal Dictionary is the documented reference: given on first world join, re-craftable from a book, green dye and any mod item [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/alexs-mobs). Maps to B19/B24 (an in-game guide item as documentation).
- Design rules on the Modrinth page: 89 mobs, mostly real animals; "no purely aesthetic creatures" (each has a drop, mechanic or function); models, textures, animations and AI are meant to match vanilla style; rare or endangered animals do not drop items only on death and many drop items when performing an action; most real-world mobs are provokable rather than hostile; spawns are "extremely configurable" by BiomeDictionary, biome registry name, dimension tag and biome category; Forge and NeoForge; 1.20.1, 1.19.4, 1.19.2, 1.19, 1.18.2, 1.16.5 [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/alexs-mobs). VF: these are explicit vanilla-feel rules (see KQ4).
- Citadel (library behind Alex's Mobs/Caves and old Ice and Fire): provides advanced entity animation, loads Tabula (.tbl) and Wavefront (.obj) models, tracks entity properties for dependents, and since 1.6.3 has a config cap on mobs spawned during chunk generation so vanilla mobs stay findable in heavy mob packs; Forge and NeoForge; 1.21.x down to 1.14.4; no 26.x listed [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/citadel). Changelog 2.7.1 (2026-07-18, 1.21-1.21.11 NeoForge) "tweaked pathfinding, collision and rendering logic"; 2.7.0 fixed a server-side memory leak in world-data loading [doc](https://api.modrinth.com/v2/project/citadel/version). The GitHub wiki has only Home and Getting-Started and notes mixins since 1.7.0 [doc](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/Citadel/wiki). Maps to B25 (spawn caps as a park-density lever). VF: neutral.

#### Alex's Caves (official wiki alexscaves.wiki.gg; Modrinth)
- Versions: Modrinth lists only 1.20.1, Forge and NeoForge, requires Citadel, last updated about two years ago; six cave biomes; wiki at alexscaves.wiki.gg [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/alexs-caves).
- Primordial Caves (B21 worldgen pattern, B18 respawn): biome temperature 0.95; ambient light "equivalent to daytime" from Ambersol so no monsters spawn (biome is in `forge:no_default_monsters`); eight inhabitants (Subterranodon, Vallumraptor, Grottoceratops, Trilocaris, Tremorsaurus, Relicheirus, Luxtructosaurus, Atlatitan); Amber Monoliths repopulate nearby animals over a few days in proportion to natural occurrence; open spaces carved by the `alexscaves:dino_bowl` structure [doc](https://alexscaves.wiki.gg/wiki/Primordial_Caves). VF: ok.

### Inferences
- [inf] F/A's documented chain (fossil 22.5% -> bio-fossil 15% DNA -> 10% vat failure -> 100 s hatch -> 6-12 day growth) is the only complete published tuning set for a dinosaur-park loop; Nublar's B05/B06/B11 TOML defaults can be benchmarked against it, with the fossil and DNA odds as the two documented grind levers.
- [inf] Three independent wikis converge on the same imprint-and-order model: a hatchling is tamed if the player is within N blocks (F/A 6, Alex's Caves 10, Ice and Fire "near"), and tamed animals cycle stay / follow / wander, with Ice and Fire adding "wander within a home position" and Cockatrice adding "patrol 50 blocks". That set is the de facto documented standard for B19.
- [inf] Documented breeding cooldowns are consistently the vanilla 5 minutes (F/A, Alex's Caves, Friends and Foes), while growth times diverge widely (Alex's Caves 1 day, F/A 6-12 days, Ice and Fire 125 days); B16 smooth growth can keep vanilla's breed cooldown and choose a species growth time by datapack without breaking any documented convention.
- [inf] Only Alex's Caves documents a clutch size range (1-4 raptor eggs) and an Elder-led pack cap (8); these are the closest published numbers for B18's raptor packs and nests.
- [inf] F/A's feeder values are the only published food-value table for dinosaurs; its 6-block detection and 2-block head reach are concrete starting values for B20 and B17 grazing reach.
- [inf] No wiki in this set documents a driven vehicle, a track/tour ride, an electric fence or a firearm; B22, B15 fences and B23 have no documentation-layer precedent among these mods and must rely on the source-code researchers.

## Key question 2: For each library (GeckoLib 5, AzureLib, SmartBrainLib 2, EMF, Citadel, TslatEntityStatus), what do the docs say about performance, culling, scheduling, scan rates, render layers, and migration to 26.x / GeckoLib 5 / SmartBrainLib 2?

### Takeaway
GeckoLib 5's documented architecture is a two-stage render pass (fill a per-object `GeoRenderState` via `addRenderData`, then render from the state only) because the animatable is no longer available on the render thread; its changelogs record the 5.5 move to the `com.geckolib` package for 26.1, the removal of `reRender`/`SerializableDataTickets`, and `CustomBoneTextureGeoLayer` as the performant replacement for Dynamic renderers, but GeckoLib publishes no culling or LOD feature. AzureLib is the only animation library in the set that documents distance LOD (bone LOD at 20 blocks / depth 3, animation LOD at 28 blocks / every 2 ticks by default) and a large-hitbox navigator, with the caveat that its changelog admits animation LOD only started working on 2026-10-09. SmartBrainLib documents sensors ticking about once per second as its core efficiency lever, and SBL 2.0.0 (2026-06-30, MC 26.2) auto-ticks the brain, auto-creates the provider, renames `BrainActivityGroup` to `ActivityBuilder`, and adds `ExtendedSensor#onlyScanIf`. TES exposes a 64-block tracking distance and a 400-tick cache clean as its performance knobs. EMF's only performance note is to pair it with Entity Culling.

### Cited Findings

#### GeckoLib (GitHub wiki, clone last commit 2026-08-09; wiki.geckolib.com; Modrinth and Modrinth API)
- Versions: Modrinth lists 26.3, 26.2, 26.1.2, 26.1, 1.21.10-1.21.11, 1.21.3-1.21.8, 1.21-1.21.1, 1.20.x and older to 1.12.2; Fabric, Forge, NeoForge, Quilt; MIT [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/geckolib). The new wiki's support table marks 5.5.7 for 26.3 as the fully supported line, 26.2-26.1 as bug-fix lines, 1.21.5-1.21.8 as covered by the old wiki, and 1.21.9 as having no GeckoLib build [doc](https://wiki.geckolib.com/docs/geckolib5/). The GitHub wiki sidebar points GeckoLib 5 readers to the new wiki [doc](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/wiki).
- GeckoLib 5 conceptual change (B09 migration): Mojang began separating the game thread from the render thread in 1.21, so the rendered object may not exist during rendering; GeckoLib 5 renderers, Molang and animation pipelines are instanced with no state carried between passes; each pass has two stages, RenderState extraction (GeckoLib fills a `GeoRenderState`, and you add data by overriding `GeoRenderer#addRenderData`) and rendering from the state only; `GeoModel`s and `GeoRenderLayer`s add data at fill time; all render data, including Molang query results, must be final before submission [doc](https://wiki.geckolib.com/docs/geckolib5/updating/important/conceptual-changes). A new `GeoRenderState` is created per render pass per object [doc](https://wiki.geckolib.com/docs/geckolib5/updating/important/renderstates). Sibling "Updating" pages: GeoModel Asset Paths, AnimationController, Generic Types, Asset Paths, RenderStates, AnimationState, RenderPassInfo, Bone Snapshots, Bone Updaters [doc](https://wiki.geckolib.com/docs/geckolib5/updating/important/conceptual-changes).

#### AzureLib (moddedmc.wiki docs; Modrinth and Modrinth API)
- Identity and versions: a branch derived from GeckoLib 4.x whose author supports AzureLib only; 26.3, 26.2, 26.1.2, 1.21-1.21.1, 1.20.x and older to 1.7.10; Fabric, Forge, NeoForge, Quilt; MIT; docs at moddedmc.wiki and wiki.azuredoom.com [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/azurelib). - Distance LOD (B09/B25): `AzEntityRendererConfig` applies LOD by default with no model changes; bone LOD hides bones deeper than a hierarchy depth past a distance (tails and fingers vanish, limbs stay); animation LOD updates the controller every N ticks past a distance and holds the pose between; `AzLodConfig.DEFAULT` is bone LOD at 20 blocks with depth 3 or less and animation LOD at 28 blocks every 2 ticks; `DISABLED` turns both off; custom tuning via `boneLod(distance, maxDepth)` and `animLod(distance, tickInterval)`; guidance: lower `maxDepth` (about 2) for horde mobs, higher for bosses, `DISABLED` for bosses that must stay full detail; an interval of 2 roughly halves animation CPU and 4 quarters it; other entity options are death rotation, shadow radius, per-bone texture and RenderType overrides, pre/post render hooks, alpha (below 1.0 switches to translucent) and scale [doc](https://moddedmc.wiki/project/azurelib/latest/docs/misc/rendering/azrenderer_config). - SmartBrainLib integration (B17/B09): the example wires `AnimatableMeleeAttack<>(delay)` with `whenStarting` calling `triggerAnim("attackController", "melee")`; the constructor argument is the tick delay before the hit lands (10 ticks = 0.5 s); a `MOVEMENT_SLOWDOWN` effect of 10 ticks can hold the mob still during the swing [doc](https://moddedmc.wiki/project/azurelib/latest/docs/entities/entity_smartbrainlib).

#### SmartBrainLib (GitHub wiki, clone last commit 2026-07-09; Modrinth and Modrinth API)
- Design claims (B17/B25): SBL replaces the vanilla brain system with its own brain, provider, memories, sensors and behaviours, built on the goals "make it fast" and "make it usable"; the author claims that with conservative sensors and behaviours an SBL entity can run more efficiently than an equivalent goal-based entity, while admitting the brain system has a larger code footprint and a steeper learning curve [doc](https://github.com/Tslat/SmartBrainLib/wiki/About). The Modrinth page repeats the claim as "Done right, an SBL entity could be even more efficient than one using goals!" and lists automatic memory-type handling, configurable sensors, behaviours with callbacks, predicates and cooldowns, and runtime add/remove of behaviours [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/smartbrainlib).

Sensors default to once per second and are configurable per `ExtendedSensor`; only `LivingEntity` subclasses can use brains; sensor and behaviour order is deterministic; the example idles for 30-60 ticks between random walks [doc](https://github.com/Tslat/SmartBrainLib/wiki/Making-an-Entity-With-SmartBrainLib).

- Versions and SBL 2 migration: Modrinth lists 26.3, 26.2, 26.1.2, 1.21.3-1.21.11, 1.21-1.21.1, 1.20.x, 1.19.x, 1.18.2, 1.16.5; Fabric, Forge, NeoForge, Quilt; MPL-2.0 [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/smartbrainlib). ## Key question 4: What design rationale or "vanilla feel" rules do the authors state?

### Takeaway
The explicit design statements cluster around one idea: new creatures should look, animate and behave as if Mojang had shipped them. Alex's Mobs states the most operational rules (every mob has a function, vanilla-matching models/animations/AI, real animals are provokable rather than hostile, rare animals yield items through actions not death); Friends and Foes frames its work as "believable vanilla plus"; Naturalist aims to fit "seamlessly into Vanilla"; Vanilla Refresh ships every feature as a toggle to preserve the vanilla experience; Fuzs's mods are small, server-side-where-possible fixes with per-side TOML configs; Mowzie's Mobs instead prioritises unique AI and animation spectacle, and Mutant Monsters explicitly aims for boss-scale danger.

### Cited Findings
- Alex's Mobs: most mobs are real animals; there are "no purely aesthetic creatures" because each has a drop, mechanic or function; models, textures, animations and AI are meant to match the vanilla style; rare or endangered animals do not drop items only on death and often drop them on an action; most real-world mobs are provokable, not hostile, while fantastical ones are hostile; built to coexist with biome, block and dimension mods [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/alexs-mobs). Maps to B16/B17/B19/B24.
- Alex's Caves: biomes are deliberately rare, each with unique blocks, items, mobs, mechanics, particles, fog and light colour; the authors recommend playing without shaders because many alter the biome look [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/alexs-caves). Documented dinosaur rules reuse vanilla idioms: wolf-style ownership and defence, turtle-style trampleable eggs with Silk Touch pickup, 5-minute breeding cooldown, creature classification that persists on Peaceful [doc](https://alexscaves.wiki.gg/wiki/Tremorsaurus), [doc](https://alexscaves.wiki.gg/wiki/Vallumraptor).
- Friends and Foes: adds outvoted and forgotten mob-vote mobs in a "believable vanilla plus style", expanding the original concepts with vanilla-like features [doc](https://github.com/Faboslav/friends-and-foes), [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/friends-and-foes).
- Naturalist: aims to "fit them seamlessly into Vanilla"; mobs interact with each other and their surroundings and some are tameable [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/naturalist); its wiki pages copy the minecraft.wiki stat-box format (health, behaviour, mob type, attack per difficulty, hitbox, speed, knockback resistance, spawn weight and group, usable items) [doc](https://github.com/starfish-studios/Naturalist/wiki/Alligator); a 2.0.4 changelog justifies blocking small mobs from Nether portals as a lag safety net [doc](https://api.modrinth.com/v2/project/naturalist/version).
- Vanilla Refresh: features aim to enhance the game while maintaining the vanilla experience, and every feature can be toggled in a settings menu [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/vanilla-refresh).
- Fuzs mods (Guard Villagers port page, Illager Invasion, Mutant Monsters, skeleton-ai-fix): shared conventions are Puzzles Lib as the base, in-game config via Mod Menu where available, manual config in `MOD_ID-client.toml`, `MOD_ID-common.toml` or `MOD_ID-server.toml`, server-side installs for behaviour fixes, and a stated lag lever (disable guard patrolling) [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/skeleton-ai-fix), [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/illager-invasion), [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/mutant-monsters), [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/guard-villagers). Mutant Monsters states its aim as taking vanilla mobs to much more dangerous heights with epic battles and rewarding loot [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/mutant-monsters).
- Mowzie's Mobs: the wiki describes the mod as priding itself on unique AI, environmental appropriateness and animation quality [doc](https://mowziesmobs.fandom.com/wiki/Mowzie%27s_Mobs_Wiki); the Modrinth FAQ notes mobs spawn by biome tags so modded biomes work when tagged [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/mowzies-mobs).
- SmartBrainLib: rationale is that vanilla's brain system is powerful but inefficient, over-abstracted and hard to use, so SBL replaces it while keeping compatibility where feasible, and recommends goals for simple mobs and brains for complex AI [doc](https://github.com/Tslat/SmartBrainLib/wiki/About).
- MCA: aims to "Bring life to those boring villages!" and profiles with YourKit [doc](https://modrinth.com/mod/minecraft-comes-alive-reborn).
- Ice and Fire CE: unofficial fork focused on optimizations, rewrites and fixes; configuration moved to an operator-gated in-game GUI and datapack biome settings [doc](https://modrinth.com/project/VpmCsizY).

### Inferences
- [inf] Measured against these stated rules, F/A's documented mechanics (iron-tier block breaking, arrow-proof hitboxes, chat spam for hunger, held-item steering, fight-to-8-HP taming, exploding culture vats) are the ones most at odds with a vanilla-feel target, while Alex's Caves' dinosaur rules are the closest published model of "vanilla plus" dinosaurs; Nublar's B15/B16/B19 defaults can be checked against both ends.
- [inf] The Naturalist and Mutant Monsters wiki stat boxes (health, per-difficulty attack, hitbox, speed, knockback resistance, spawn weight/group, biome tags with blacklist tags) are a ready documentation template for Nublar's species pages and for the fields a species JSON should expose.

### Gaps
- Guard Villagers' and Better Villages' authors publish no design philosophy beyond feature lists.
- Fossils and Archeology Revival's wiki contains no author design statement; its "Upcoming Content" page (referenced by the Bio-Fossil page as replacing the fossil system) was not read [doc](https://fossilsarcheology.wiki.gg/wiki/Bio-Fossil).
- EMF, GeckoLib, AzureLib, TES, boids and cosy-critters state no gameplay design rationale.
