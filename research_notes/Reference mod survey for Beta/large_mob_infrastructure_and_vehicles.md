## Key question 1: How do Alex's Mobs, Alex's Caves and Citadel keep many mobs and huge bosses cheap, what does Citadel offer, and what do the issue trackers say?

### Takeaway
These mods are not performance-engineered; they rely on vanilla goal gating (random chance plus `noActionTime`), a handful of `tickCount % N` intervals, per-species despawn overrides and config toggles, and they pay for it with a long list of open TPS/FPS issues. Citadel's shared layer adds global per-entity-per-tick and per-render hooks (tick-rate check, four render events, an entity-data compound) that cost every entity in the world, not just Alex's. The useful transferable patterns are: distance-gated rendering (`shouldRenderAtSqrDistance`, inflated culling boxes, per-part frustum tests), dormant bosses, cached block scans keyed on block position, config-driven spawn rules, and AzureLib's render LOD (see question 4).

## Key question 2: How does Alex's Caves implement vehicles, guns, machines and energy?

### Takeaway
The submarine is a plain `Entity` driven on the controlling client with vanilla-style lerp on other sides, three keybinds forwarded by a validated packet, and rider state in synced data; the raygun is a held-item "use" loop that raycasts every tick on both sides and stores charge in item NBT with client-authoritative sync; the nuclear furnace is a 2×2×2 multiblock assembled on placement of the eighth block, with a vanilla `BaseContainerBlockEntity`, hopper side slots, waste tiers and a per-tick block-update sync. Alex's Caves has **no energy API** (no FE, no cables); heat/fuel is item-based. The only track-following code is a mag-lev variant of the vanilla minecart rail follower.

## Key question 3: How does Mowzie's Mobs drive combat from server-side animation state?

### Takeaway
Two layers coexist. The older one is Citadel-style: an `Animation` is a duration, the server picks it and broadcasts an index, and `AnimationAI` goals read the server tick counter to deal damage on a fixed frame. The newer `Ability` system replaces goals with a per-entity capability holding a map of abilities, each a track of STARTUP/ACTIVE/RECOVERY sections; the server validates `canUse`, activates, and sends a 2-varint packet to tracking clients; both sides tick the same deterministic section timers, and the client alone plays the GeckoLib animation. Hit windows are either a single ACTIVE instant (melee) or a tick range inside a section; player warnings are long startup sections and roars with camera shake.

## Key question 4: What do Citadel and AzureLib provide as infrastructure, what did AzureLib change from GeckoLib, and which fits a 26.2 multiloader dinosaur mod?

### Takeaway
Citadel (question 1) is a NeoForge-only grab-bag: code-keyframe animation with a server tick counter, a global entity-data compound, tick-rate modifiers, spawn/surface-rule/village helpers, and Patreon perks; its costs are global and its loader coverage rules it out. AzureLib 4.0.0 is a MultiLoader (Fabric + NeoForge, Java 25) MIT fork of GeckoLib 4 with a rewritten animation API: no animatable interface or cache on the entity (mixins attach an animator), server→client `AzCommand` action lists, its own config library, and a render LOD manager (bone-depth culling and animation tick skipping by distance). GeckoLib 5.5.6 is MIT, ships Forge + Fabric + NeoForge, has six 26.2 releases to AzureLib's one, and offers triggered and "stateless" play/stop packets but no LOD. Facts only: Nublar's plan already names GeckoLib 5; AzureLib's unique extra is the LOD manager, which is ~120 lines reimplementable with the JDK and GeckoLib's bone tree.

## Key question 5: Taming, riding, command and detection patterns, and how the mods avoid breaking vanilla

### Takeaway
Taming is either vanilla `TamableAnimal` plus a tag of taming items (elephant) or a counted feeding loop (Tremorzilla: four waste drums then a 1-in-3 roll per feed); commands are a small integer cycled by owner interaction with an on-screen translation message (crow: wander/follow/sit/perch, eagle: 3 states), and "sit" always routes through vanilla `setOrderedToSit`. Riding uses vanilla `getRiddenInput`/`tickRidden`/`getRiddenSpeed` with yaw copied from the player, plus one or more keybinds sent through the validated mount-key packet. Player detection is almost entirely vanilla targeting with line of sight; light level appears only in spawn rules and one mob's behaviour, and sneaking only as a dismount or grab exemption. Vanilla is protected by biome modifiers/tags, config toggles for every cross-mob interaction, and mob categories.

### Inferences
- [inf] For B19, the convergent design across these mods is: tame = vanilla `TamableAnimal` semantics (owner UUID, `setOrderedToSit`) with a species-specific tame test from datapack items; commands = one synced int with action-bar feedback; follow/defend = vanilla owner goals; every remote-control packet validated for ownership and passenger status (AC does, AM's eagle does not).
- [inf] Riding a dinosaur later (post-Beta) should follow the Tremorzilla/elephant pattern (vanilla `tickRidden` + the key packet) rather than AC's submarine entity pattern, because the mob keeps its brain for driverless behaviour.
