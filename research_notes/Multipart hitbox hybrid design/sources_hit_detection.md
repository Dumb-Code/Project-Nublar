## Q1. How does Alex's Caves compute its procedural chain, and how close do the parts stay to the rendered model? Any player reports of mismatch?

### Takeaway
The sauropod chain is a fixed list of local offsets (in blocks, multiplied by `getScale()`), placed segment by segment from the body centre. Each segment uses one smoothed pitch and a cumulative yaw. The yaw lag comes from a 128-entry `yBodyRot` ring buffer read 10 ticks back (neck) and 20 ticks back (tail). Animations act only through hand-written target angles keyed on the Citadel animation ID and tick, approached at a capped rate in degrees per tick. The model is then posed **from** the parts, through hand-tuned gains, so the rendered neck and tail follow the hitboxes by construction but not exactly. No public report of a part/model mismatch was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Ring buffer.** `yawBuffer = new float[128]` (L97). On the first tick every slot is filled with `yBodyRot`. After that the pointer advances and wraps at 128, and the current `yBodyRot` is stored each tick (L247-256). `getYawFromBuffer(pointer, partialTick)` reads `yawPointer - pointer & 127` and the sample before it and lerps between them (L434-440). — [AC SauropodBaseEntity.java L97, L247-256, L434-440](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/server/entity/living/SauropodBaseEntity.java#L247-L305)
- **Smoothing.** Four angles (`neckXRot`, `neckYRot`, `tailXRot`, `tailYRot`) move toward their targets with `Mth.approachDegrees(current, target, speed)` once per tick. A `turningFast` flag adds 30 to both speeds (L262-271). — same file L262-271
- **Rotate speeds (degrees per tick).** Neck: 10 by default, 30 during ROAR (first 50 ticks), 2 during SUMMON, 40 during SPEW_FLAMES (first 70 ticks), 30 during LEFT/RIGHT_WHIP (L481-495). Tail: 30 during whips, otherwise 10 (L523-525). — [AC SauropodBaseEntity.java L481-525](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/server/entity/living/SauropodBaseEntity.java#L481-L525)
- **Per-animation targets (degrees).** Code reading of `getTargetNeckXRot`, `getTargetNeckYRot`, `getTargetTailXRot` and `getTargetTailYRot` (L442-521):

  | State | Neck pitch | Neck yaw | Tail pitch | Tail yaw |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Default | -30 | `(yHeadRot - yBodyRot) + (buffer[10] - yBodyRot)` | 0 | `buffer[20] - yBodyRot` |
  | ROAR, tick < 50 | -140 | `sin(tick·0.2)·40` | -20 | default |
  | EPIC_DEATH, tick < 110 | -140 | `sin(tick·0.1)·20` | -20 | default |
  | SUMMON | -60 (tick < 70) | 110 (tick < 50) | -100 | default |
  | STOMP, tick ≤ 30 | 30 | default | default | default |
  | SPEW_FLAMES, tick < 70 | `60 + sin(tick·0.4)·10` | `sin(tick·0.15)·40` | default | default |
  | Dancing | 30 | default | default | default |
  | LEFT_WHIP | default | default | 20, then -20 after tick 20 | -70, then +70 after tick 24 |
  | RIGHT_WHIP | default | default | 20, then -20 after tick 20 | +70, then -70 after tick 24 |

  — [AC SauropodBaseEntity.java L442-521](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/server/entity/living/SauropodBaseEntity.java#L442-L521)
- **Placement maths.** Body centre = `position + (0, bbHeight/2 - legSolverBodyOffset, 0)`. The neck pitch is split into 4 equal steps (`neckXRot/4`) and the tail pitch into 3. The **same** pitch step is applied to every segment, while the yaw step is cumulative (`yBodyRot + headYStep × k` for segment k). Each segment centre = previous centre + `rotateOffsetVec(offset × scale, pitchStep, yaw_k)`, where `rotateOffsetVec` is `offset.xRot(-x°).yRot(-y°)`. Offsets in blocks: neck1 (0, 2, 5), neck2 (0, 0, 2.5), neck3 (0, 0, 2), head (0, 0, 2.5); tail1 (0, -0.5, -3.5), tail2 (0, -0.25, -3.25), tail3 (0, 0, -2.5). STOMP and dancing add up to +4 Y and -4 Z to neck1, and dancing also fades the neck yaw out. — [AC SauropodBaseEntity.java L272-295, L371-373](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/server/entity/living/SauropodBaseEntity.java#L272-L295)
- **Interpolation.** Positions before the move are saved and written back into `xo/yo/zo` and `xOld/yOld/zOld` after the move (L257-260, L297-304). — same file
- **Both sides.** `tickMultipart()` is called from `tick()` with no side check (L166). — [AC SauropodBaseEntity.java L166](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/server/entity/living/SauropodBaseEntity.java#L166)
- **Model reads the parts, with gains.** `positionNeckAndTail` takes `calculateAnimationAngle` of each part. It applies neck pitches × 0.5, adds fixed bias angles to the tail pitches (+0.141 rad and +0.076 rad), limits the head-yaw catch-up to 45° (`approachDegrees(..., 45F)`), and adds `Mth.clamp(headPitch, -30, 30)` to the head. All of this is **added** to the bone rotations that the Citadel keyframe animator has already set (`animate(entity)` runs first, L352; this method runs at L367). — [AC SauropodBaseModel.java L432-461](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/client/model/SauropodBaseModel.java#L432-L461)
- `calculateAnimationAngle` builds a vector from a point one part-width "behind" the part centre to the connected part's centre, and returns its pitch, or its yaw plus the lerped parent body yaw. — [AC SauropodPartEntity.java L127-152](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/blob/0ab3c214b72c846c2c1ea713a37e2b4b3a00a9f5/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/server/entity/living/SauropodPartEntity.java#L127-L152)
- **Player reports.** GitHub issue searches of AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves for "hitbox", "hit box", "neck", "can't hit" and "tremorzilla hitbox" (2026-10-09) return no report that a sauropod or Tremorzilla part box sits away from the visible neck or tail. Hitbox-related issues are about other things: #866 (baby Tremorzilla swim box, closed), #831 (heat ray misses giant mobs, closed), #391 (baby box with another mod), #1281 (a block), #1753 (1.21 port regressions, including a caramel cube hitbox method that nothing calls). — [AC issue search "hitbox"](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/issues?q=hitbox); [#1753](https://github.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/issues/1753)
- Alex's Caves has branches `1.21.1`, `candy`, `eruption` and `main`, and no 26.x branch. — [AC branches (GitHub API)](https://api.github.com/repos/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/branches)

### Inferences
- The neck shape is "a straight line pitched by `neckXRot/4`, bent sideways by cumulative yaw". At the ROAR target (-140°) each segment is pitched by 35°, so the neck hitboxes rise as a straight 35° ramp, while the model may curl further through the keyframes plus 0.5× gains. The hitboxes are therefore a coarse envelope during roars and whips, and closest during walking.
- The agreement between hitboxes and the model is "the model follows the parts". This is the only approach in the seven sources where the visual and the hitbox cannot drift apart over time, because both come from one state. Errors are bounded by the gains and by the keyframe rotations added on top.
- The pattern ports directly to the candidate spine: a ring buffer of `yBodyRot`, rate-limited angles toward per-animation targets, and offsets × growth scale. The Project Nublar variant would key targets on its own synced animation state (GeckoLib animation name and tick, or an enum in synched data) instead of Citadel's.
- Missing player reports fit the design: the visible neck is driven from the parts, so players have little to notice. It is not proof of accuracy, because AC's older part classes also drop projectile damage (earlier note), which players may not attribute to hitboxes.

### Gaps
- No in-game measurement of the distance between part centres and rendered bone pivots exists in public sources.
- The code that sets `turningFast` was not traced.
- The CurseForge comments and the Discord for Alex's Caves were not searched.

## Q2. How do the vanilla dragon and NeoForge PartEntity work on 1.21.x and 26.x? Did the MC-158205 fix change how part IDs work? Does vanilla offer anything new for multipart or entity attachments?

### Takeaway
The 24w44a fix (released in 1.21.4) changed two things. (1) Vanilla now keeps a **level-wide part list** (`Level.dragonParts()`) and scans it in the untyped `Level.getEntities`, so parts are found even when the dragon's own chunk section is outside the query. This is the same design Forge used since 1.x. (2) Third-party mirrors of 1.21.11 and 26.x show `recreateFromPacket` assigning `i + packet.getId() + 1`, which is the same `parentId + i + 1` convention MoreHitboxes uses. NeoForge 26.2's `PartEntity` is unchanged (29 lines). NeoForge now reuses vanilla's part list instead of keeping its own map. On the server, attacks resolve parts through `getEntityOrPart` and check reach against the **part's** box with a 3-block buffer. Vanilla adds nothing new for multipart: `EntityAttachments` are four fixed point types. The general large-hitbox section bug MC-261638 is still open.

### Inferences
- **For NeoForge 26.2 the "vanilla plumbing" is now complete for parts.** It covers level-wide lookup, `getEntityOrPart`, tracking exclusion, item-effect redirect and the camera fix. What NeoForge still does not do is align part IDs between the two sides. Each mod must do that, as vanilla does for the dragon.
- Because NeoForge registers parts **by their ID at tracking start**, a mod must (a) set part IDs before the parent starts tracking, and (b) never swap part objects or IDs while the parent is tracked. Otherwise `getEntityOrPart` returns null or a stale part. (IAFCE re-registers its parts manually after a rebuild, which shows the problem is real; see Q6.)
- **Parts are the cheapest fix for MC-261638 on big dinosaurs.** A long main box that spans sections can still miss melee and arrows. Parts held in the level-wide list are found regardless of sections. A long dinosaur therefore benefits from parts even for its torso.
- The 26.x server reach check validates against the **server** copy of the part box. A system whose server parts stay at rest pose while the client shows animated parts will accept or reject hits by the rest pose, with a 3-block buffer. This is one concrete reason why the server needs at least an approximate animated pose.
- Old Alex's-style relays that send packets from client-side `hurt` must move to `hurtClient` on 1.21.2+. With aligned IDs no relay is needed.
- Nothing in vanilla can replace the parts. `EntityAttachments` are static per-type points, useful for rider seats and name tags only.

### Gaps
- The exact 24w44a diff is not published. The dates (patch present in NeoForge 1.21.4, absent in 1.21.3) and the MC-158205 fix version support the attribution, but it is an inference.
- The `+ 1` ID code was read only from third-party mirrors of 1.21.11 and 26.x. No official Mojang source exists for this.
- Whether `EntitySectionStorage` queries are inflated for large entities on 26.2 (and whether NeoForge's `getMaxEntityRadius` is used anywhere) was not checked.

## Q3. What is the Fabric story? How do Fabric mods make parts visible to `Level.getEntities`, picking and attack packets?

### Takeaway
Fabric API has no multipart API, and none was proposed. Vanilla's own part list is typed `EnderDragonPart`, whose constructor needs an `EnderDragon`, so a Fabric mod cannot reuse it. Fabric mods therefore copy NeoForge's hooks with mixins. MoreHitboxes' Fabric module (1.21.1) is the most complete public copy: a level-wide part map on `ServerLevel` and `ClientLevel`, `RETURN` injects into both `Level.getEntities` variants, a `TAIL` inject into `ServerLevel.getEntityOrPart`, tracking callbacks, a `ChunkMap.addEntity` cancel, a reach override and a camera redirect. Items (1)-(4) decide hit detection. Items (5)-(7) are polish. MoreHitboxes' Fabric module covers 1-4 and 6 directly; its common `PlayerMixin` covers attack handling.
- The typed-query choice matters. MoreHitboxes adds the **parent** for typed queries (so `getEntitiesOfClass(LivingEntity.class, …)` sees the dinosaur when only its tail overlaps), while NeoForge adds the **part** if `tryCast` accepts it (a `PartEntity` is not a `LivingEntity`, so such queries miss it). The two loaders therefore behave differently for area damage unless the hybrid fixes one rule on both (for example, also resolve parent hits in its own AoE code).
- Biome Makeover's design would re-introduce the MC-261638-type miss for long dinosaurs, because a part is found only if the parent's section was queried. It is not a good model.
- The MoreHitboxes `ServerPlayerMixin` redirect assigns `camera` only when the current camera is a part or the field is null. Read literally, a second `setCamera` call to a normal entity could leave the old camera in place. This was not tested; treat it as a possible bug and do not copy it as is.
- No Fabric mod found uses Fabric's data attachment API for parts. Countered 1.0.1 used an attachment only for client vertex storage.

## Q4. MoreHitboxes: current status, Fabric support, server-side override, GeckoLib 5

### Takeaway
MoreHitboxes has not moved since 2026-03-31. The newest builds are `1.21.1-1.9.4-alpha` for NeoForge and Fabric. There is no 1.21.2+ or 26.x build and no GeckoLib 5 support. ### Inferences
- **A server-side procedural override works with MoreHitboxes' code as written**: compute the section offset (already rotated and scaled, in world axes) and call `part.setOverride(new AnimationOverride(offset, 1, 1))` on the server each tick, **before** `super.aiStep()` returns, or the box lags one tick. On the client the renderer mixin then overwrites it with the bone, which gives exactly the candidate's "server procedural, client refines from bones" split. But the dependency is stuck on 1.21.1 / GeckoLib 4. For 26.2 the hybrid must own this logic.

- GeckoLib 5's statement that bones have a position only at render time confirms there is no supported server-side pose. Server truth must stay procedural (or a separately written evaluator).

## Q6. Damage routing done safely: reach checks, which part wins, per-part multipliers, keeping the attacker

### Takeaway
With aligned part IDs, vanilla already does the safe thing on 26.2. The client picks the **nearest** box along the view ray. It sends the part's ID. The server resolves it with `getEntityOrPart`, checks reach against that part's server box with a 3-block buffer, and calls `player.attack(part)`. The part's `hurtServer` then forwards the **same** `DamageSource` (attacker kept) to the parent, with an optional multiplier. Parent invulnerability frames make several parts hit by one explosion count once, at the highest amount. Every custom client-to-server relay in the sources weakened at least one of these checks.

### Cited Findings
**Which hit wins**
- Crosshair picking (`ProjectileUtil.getEntityHitResult(Entity, from, to, box, predicate, maxDist)`) loops `level.getEntities(...)`, which includes parts. It clips each box inflated by `getPickRadius()` and keeps the nearest intersection, with a box that contains the eye winning at distance 0. Projectile picking (`getEntityHitResult(Level, source, …, margin)`) does the same with a margin from 0 to 0.3 by projectile age. — [V262 ProjectileUtil.java L109-145, L153-182](https://github.com/Renekovski/26.2-mcp/blob/51f3128ba7265299dce20b9abd2b37d7dd27d096/src/net/minecraft/world/entity/projectile/ProjectileUtil.java#L109-L182)
- MoreHitboxes rewraps a part hit as an `EntityHitResult(parent)` carrying the part, so code that checks "is this my target" sees the parent while the attack still goes to the part. — [MH21 ProjectileUtilOverride.java](https://github.com/DarkPred/MoreHitboxes/blob/88899b3e1c4ed881b1ea65b35104453fc6df592a/common/src/main/java/com/github/darkpred/morehitboxes/internal/ProjectileUtilOverride.java)
- CustomHitboxLib and Countered 1.0.1 replace the picking function and return the parent only, so the identity of the hit part is lost (earlier notes).

**Reach**
- Vanilla 26.2 checks reach against the resolved target box (the part's box when a part ID was sent): `isWithinAttackRange(weapon, targetBounds, 3.0)` for attacks and `isWithinEntityInteractionRange(targetBounds, 3.0)` for interaction. — [V262 ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.java L1785-1836](https://github.com/Renekovski/26.2-mcp/blob/51f3128ba7265299dce20b9abd2b37d7dd27d096/src/net/minecraft/server/network/ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.java#L1785-L1836)
- CustomHitboxLib had to add an "any part box in range" reach rule after issue #2 (parts about 4 blocks from the centre failed reach), because its hits resolve to the parent's box. MoreHitboxes on Fabric widens interact reach to 6/9 blocks against the target or part box. — [CustomHitboxLib issue #2](https://github.com/gawrmonster/CustomHitboxLib/issues/2); [MH21 ServerGamePacketListenerImplMixin.java L22-37](https://github.com/DarkPred/MoreHitboxes/blob/88899b3e1c4ed881b1ea65b35104453fc6df592a/fabric/src/main/java/com/github/darkpred/morehitboxes/mixin/ServerGamePacketListenerImplMixin.java#L22-L37)
- Unsafe relays in the sources (earlier notes): Alex's Mobs' `MessageHurtMultipart` trusts a client damage amount with no reach check; Alex's Caves' `MultipartEntityMessage` accepts any multipart parent within 16 blocks; Ice and Fire 1.20 messages act within 100 blocks.

**Multipliers and attacker**
- Vanilla dragon: the current phase's `onHurt` runs first, then non-head parts take `damage/4 + min(damage, 1)`, damage under 0.01 is dropped, and the source is passed through unchanged to `reallyHurt` → `LivingEntity.hurtServer`. A hit on the dragon's own box routes as a body hit. — [V262 EnderDragon.java L444-477](https://github.com/Renekovski/26.2-mcp/blob/51f3128ba7265299dce20b9abd2b37d7dd27d096/src/net/minecraft/world/entity/boss/enderdragon/EnderDragon.java#L444-L477)
- Ice and Fire CE (26.1.2): `MultipartPartEntity.hurtServer` returns false for FALL, DROWN, IN_WALL, FALLING_BLOCK, LAVA and the IS_FIRE tag, and otherwise calls `parent.hurtServer(level, source, damage * damageMultiplier)`. `interact` forwards to the parent. — [IAFCE MultipartPartEntity.java](https://github.com/IAFEnvoy/IceAndFire-CE/blob/63a3bba7960ad991c67fe1d1f4ea1c32e6523741/src/main/java/com/iafenvoy/iceandfire/entity/MultipartPartEntity.java)
- MoreHitboxes passes the part to `partHurt(part, source, amount)` and exposes `getPartName()` for multipliers (earlier note).

**Repeat hits**
- `LivingEntity.hurtServer`: while `invulnerableTime > 10` and the source does not bypass cooldown, a new hit only applies `damage - lastHurt`, and only if it is larger than `lastHurt`. Otherwise it sets `invulnerableTime = 20`. — [V262 LivingEntity.java L1194-1208](https://github.com/Renekovski/26.2-mcp/blob/51f3128ba7265299dce20b9abd2b37d7dd27d096/src/net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java#L1194-L1208)

**ID robustness (new pattern)**
- Ice and Fire CE reserves a contiguous ID block for a parent and all its parts from the global counter: `counter.getAndAdd(partCount + 1) + 1` (through an accessor mixin), and calls `setId(reserveParentId(getParts().length))` in the constructor. Its `setId` override sets `parts[i].setId(getId() + i + 1)`. On scale change it **reuses** part objects (it creates one only if it is null or removed). Its `registerParts` / `unregisterParts` write directly into the server part map (through an accessor) "to keep server-side ray tracing and interaction packets aware of all dragon parts". The commit that moved it to native parts is `ef10e277` (2026-08-18, "use native multipart entities and full dragon collision bounds"). — [IAFCE MultipartPartEntity.java (reserveParentId)](https://github.com/IAFEnvoy/IceAndFire-CE/blob/63a3bba7960ad991c67fe1d1f4ea1c32e6523741/src/main/java/com/iafenvoy/iceandfire/entity/MultipartPartEntity.java); [IAFCE DragonBaseEntity.java L269, L339-392, L454-492](https://github.com/IAFEnvoy/IceAndFire-CE/blob/63a3bba7960ad991c67fe1d1f4ea1c32e6523741/src/main/java/com/iafenvoy/iceandfire/entity/DragonBaseEntity.java#L454-L492); [IAFCE commits](https://github.com/IAFEnvoy/IceAndFire-CE/commits/master)
- Ice and Fire CE also adds a `BodyPartEntity` that mirrors the parent's own box, because "mods that treat the parts of a multipart entity as the complete set of hitboxes … never test the parent's own box". — [IAFCE BodyPartEntity.java](https://github.com/IAFEnvoy/IceAndFire-CE/blob/63a3bba7960ad991c67fe1d1f4ea1c32e6523741/src/main/java/com/iafenvoy/iceandfire/entity/BodyPartEntity.java)

### Inferences
- **Safe routing recipe** (all pieces proven in the sources):
  1. Aligned IDs: reserve a contiguous block in the constructor (IAFCE) and set `parentId + i + 1` in a `setId` override (vanilla 26.x dragon, MoreHitboxes, IAFCE). Reserving the block removes the risk that another entity built on another thread takes an ID between the parent and its parts. With aligned IDs no custom packet is needed, so none of the relay exploits apply.
  2. Server reach: use vanilla's check against the server part box. It is only meaningful if the server part is near where the player saw it, which argues for procedural server placement over a rest pose.
  3. Nearest hit wins: vanilla's ray picking already gives the closest part along the ray. No extra rule is needed. Keep the parent's own box pickable only if it does not overlap a part in a way that steals hits (the dragon makes its parent non-pickable). An alternative is IAFCE's body part.
  4. Multipliers: one table per species keyed by section name (head, neck, torso, tail), applied in the part's `hurtServer` before forwarding. Forward the original `DamageSource` object. This keeps the attacker, kill credit, enchantment effects and retaliation, which Project Nublar's current `hurtFromPart` loses (earlier report, defect 6).
  5. Filter environmental damage at the part (fall, drown, in-wall, lava, fire), as IAFCE does, so a tail clipping into lava does not burn the dinosaur.
  6. AoE: parent invulnerability frames already collapse multi-part hits to the largest one.
- If parts are rebuilt (growth), keep the same objects and only call `refreshDimensions()`, as both AC and IAFCE do. Then the ID map stays valid without re-registration.

### Gaps
- `isWithinAttackRange` internals (the weapon `AttackRange` component on 26.x) were not read. Whether a spear or other piercing weapon bypasses the part path (the code skips `player.attack` when the item has `PIERCING_WEAPON`) was not traced.

## Q7. Which pieces are proven, new or in conflict? Does anything in these sources beat the candidate hybrid for hit detection?

### Takeaway
Nothing in the sources beats the candidate for hit detection on two loaders. The candidate is the vanilla-dragon / Alex's Caves design (deterministic procedural parts on both sides) scaled by growth. Its weak points are fidelity to keyframed poses and the plumbing (IDs, Fabric hooks). Fidelity can be raised by hand-tuned per-animation targets (AC) or by having the client model follow the parts (AC). The plumbing can be copied from vanilla 26.2 + NeoForge (NeoForge side) and MoreHitboxes' Fabric module (Fabric side). Client bone refinement through GeckoLib 5 listeners is optional. It improves melee picking but reintroduces a client/server split for projectiles and reach. The only "better" alternatives (oriented-box or triangle narrow phase, server-side model posing) cost more than they return.

### Inferences
**Proven** (shipped in vanilla or several mods): B1-B4, B6, B11, B14, B15. **New or single-source**: B5 (IAFCE, weeks old), B7-B8 (AC only), B9 (GeckoLib 5 API exists, no studied mod uses it for hitboxes yet), B10 (exists, never used server-side). **Unproven at scale**: B16, B17.

**Conflicts to resolve in the design**
- *Client refinement (B9) vs server truth (B6).* If the client moves a part to the bone while the server keeps the procedural spot, melee follows the client, reach is checked against the server box (3-block buffer), and arrows hit the server box. That is the MoreHitboxes split, made smaller. Options: (a) no client refinement for gameplay: client parts = server procedural parts, and the bone capture is used only for a debug overlay that shows "procedural vs bone" error while tuning B7 targets; (b) clamp the client offset to within about 1 block of the procedural position. Option (a) is the most reliable. Option (b) improves feel and keeps reach checks valid.
- *B8 vs GeckoLib keyframes.* AC can pose the model from parts because Citadel models are code-animated. In GeckoLib, keyframed neck and tail bones already move. Driving them from parts as well means adding an offset with a bone updater (GeckoLib 5 `addBoneUpdater`), and the keyframes then need to keep neck/tail motion small. This is a content-pipeline decision, not a code risk.
- *Typed-query semantics (Q3).* NeoForge returns parts only to queries that accept `PartEntity`; MoreHitboxes Fabric returns the parent. The hybrid should pick one rule and implement it on both loaders, or else write its own AoE code to resolve parts to parents.
- *Parent box pickable vs torso part.* The vanilla dragon makes the parent non-pickable; IAFCE adds a body part that mirrors the parent box. For Project Nublar, keeping the parent pickable as the torso (and making the torso section part optional) avoids double coverage. If the torso is a part (to escape MC-261638 for very long bodies), make the parent non-pickable.
- *Growth.* Placement offsets and part sizes must both scale with the growth scale (AC multiplies offsets by `getScale()` and sizes through `size.scale(parent.getScale())`, per the earlier note). Keep part objects stable across growth.

**Does anything beat the candidate?**
- *Oriented boxes or triangles (B16)* fit diagonal necks better than a chain of axis-aligned cubes. But they need a replaced `ProjectileUtil` on both sides (Countered and CustomHitboxLib both replace it), lose part identity unless redesigned, and conflict with other mods hooking the same method. The candidate's chain of small near-cubes reaches similar accuracy for 2-3 boxes per section, using unmodified vanilla picking. Not better on balance.
- *Server-side posing (B17)* gives exact server truth. GeckoLib 5 states bones have positions only at render time, so this means writing and maintaining an animation evaluator. The old Project Nublar churned on its cost. Not better.
- *A capsule per section (narrow phase only).* No studied source does this for hits. It would need the same `ProjectileUtil` hook as B16. Not recommended for hit detection. It remains a good fit for herd spacing and turn clearance, which share the spine but not the hit path.

**Judgement: most reliable combination**
1. Spine + section parts placed by the same deterministic function on both sides (B6), with a `yBodyRot` ring buffer for tail lag, `approachDegrees` smoothing, per-animation targets keyed on synced animation state (B7), and growth scale on offsets and sizes. Save pre-move positions into `xo/xOld` for interpolation.
2. Parts: `PartEntity` on NeoForge (B1-B3, B14 native). On Fabric, a common-interface part class extending `Entity` plus the MoreHitboxes-style mixin set (B1-B3, B14 copied), level-wide list included.
3. IDs: reserve a contiguous block in the constructor (B5) and override `setId` to `parentId + i + 1` (B4) in common code. Create parts in the constructor and never replace them.
4. Damage: part `hurtServer` filters environmental sources and forwards the same `DamageSource` × per-section multiplier to the parent (B11). No custom network relay.
5. Client: no gameplay refinement at first. Use GeckoLib 5 bone listeners (B9) only for a debug comparison while tuning targets. Optional later: clamped client refinement.
6. Culling bounds from the part union (B15).

This uses only patterns that shipped in vanilla, AC, IAFCE or MoreHitboxes, needs no dependency beyond GeckoLib, and gives the server an approximate animated pose that is good enough for vanilla's 3-block reach buffer and for arrows.

### Gaps
- No studied source publishes measured part/bone error for any animation, so the accuracy of "procedural targets" for Project Nublar's GeckoLib animations is untested.
- No profiling data exists for vanilla's level-wide part loop with hundreds of parts (for example a herd of 20 Brachiosaurus × 6 parts = 120 parts scanned on every `getEntities` call in that level).
- GeckoLib 5 bone updaters were not checked for whether they can add offsets on top of keyframed bones without fighting the animation controller.
