# GeckoLib animation features and the Blockbench authoring pipeline

Research note for the GeckoLib creature animation survey. Written 2026-11-03. Scope: what GeckoLib's animation system offers, version by version (4.7.3 that Nublar ships, 4.8.4 at the head of the 1.20.1 branch, and 5.5.6 on the 26.2 branch for the port), how to add procedural motion on top of keyframes, what the Blockbench export pipeline does to keyframe density, and the known traps. AzureLib (a GeckoLib fork) is read only for features GeckoLib lacks. The render pipeline and per-bone draw cost of GeckoLib 5 are in `research_notes/Reference mod survey for Beta/client_rendering_and_animation.md` (Key question 3) and are not repeated here. Nublar's own six animation files were measured with a read-only Python script.

## Key question 1: Every animation feature, 4.x vs 5.x

### Takeaway

Both versions have the same authoring vocabulary: three loop types, `RawAnimation` chains (play, loop, wait, hold, play N times), server-triggered animations, sound, particle and custom-instruction keyframe markers, a per-controller transition length, a per-controller speed, an easing override and 30+ easings with catmull-rom. The engines under them differ a lot. GeckoLib 4 scans every keyframe list from the start on every frame, plays time as speed times elapsed time, lets the last controller overwrite each keyed transform, has no additive mode, and knows 15 Molang queries. GeckoLib 5 walks keyframe indices forward incrementally, integrates speed per frame, adds `additiveAnimations()`, inserts transition stages between chain steps and a reset stage at the end, and knows about 80 queries computed only when an animation uses them. Neither version plays `bezier` easing, blend weights, `anim_time_update`, `blend_weight` or bone masks; AzureLib does weights, masks and bezier.

### Cited Findings

**Loop types**

- 4.x registers `play_once` (and `false`), `hold_on_last_frame`, `loop` (and `true`), plus a `DEFAULT` that defers to the file's loop value; unknown names fall back to play once. Hold works by setting the controller state to PAUSED and returning "play again". [v] [GL4C animation/Animation.java L39-46, L64-98](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/core/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/core/animation/Animation.java#L39-L98)
- 5.x keeps the same names. Hold now pins the timeline time to the stage end; loop subtracts the stage length so overshoot carries into the next cycle (no lost fraction of a frame at the seam). [v] [GL5 animation/object/LoopType.java L20-33](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animation/object/LoopType.java#L20-L33)
- 4.x `hasAnimationFinished` is true only in the STOPPED state, so a held animation (PAUSED) never reports finished. 5.x reports finished once the point reaches the animation length and the timeline is at its last end. [v] [GL4C AnimationController L315-317](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/core/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/core/animation/AnimationController.java#L315-L317); [GL5 AnimationController L156-164](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animation/AnimationController.java#L156-L164)

**Transition length**

- 4.x: on a new animation the controller enters TRANSITIONING; for `transitionLength` ticks of (speed-scaled) time each keyed bone lerps from the saved snapshot of its current pose to the new animation's value at time 0; then the animation starts from 0. **Easings and how they evaluate**

- Both versions: `linear`, `step` (argument = step count, at least 2), and in/out/in-out of sine, quad, cubic, quart, quint, expo, circ, back, elastic, bounce, plus `catmullrom`. ## Key question 2: Procedural bone deltas and head look

### Takeaway

In 4.x, write procedural motion in `GeoModel#setCustomAnimations`, which runs once per entity per frame right after the controllers. Bones are shared by every entity of that model, so always write absolute values from this frame's inputs and never read last frame's value as state. In 5.x, write it in the renderer's `adjustModelBonesForRender(renderPassInfo, snapshots)`: it runs after the controllers on a fresh per-pass snapshot, so adding to the snapshot is safe. GeckoLib's own head look is a two-line overwrite of the head bone's pitch and yaw with no clamp, no smoothing, no neck chain and no blend with the animation. Build Nublar's own look-at on top instead.

- [inf] Doing head look in Molang (5.x query) puts it inside the animation and lets transitions blend it. But a `query.head_y_rotation` keyframe only applies while that animation plays, and two controllers listing the neck still fight. A delta in the updater is easier to layer.

## Key question 3: Authoring and performance of densely baked keyframes

### Takeaway

Nublar's files are not dense everywhere. They mix sparse hand keys (catmull-rom, about 2 keys per second per channel) with baked channels at 24-25 samples per second, typical of Blockbench IK baking, bezier baking or the Bakery plugin. 110,620 keyframes sit in 8,925 channels. 64% of channels are constant and 58% are exactly the rest pose: 1,432 of 4,545 bone entries animate nothing. A tolerance-based reduction that keeps the curve within 0.1° and 0.01 units removes about 40% of keys; within 0.5° and 0.05 units, about 60%. Removing rest-pose channels is exact and also stops them from masking other controllers. 178 keys hold rotation values of 10^7 to 10^10 degrees, almost certainly corrupt. On 4.x the per-frame cost grows with keyframe count, because every axis is scanned from its first key every frame, and held poses scan the whole list. On 5.x the per-frame cost is nearly independent of density; density then costs only memory and load time.

### Cited Findings

- The 4.x catmull start-tangent error versus correct catmull, on 1,481 catmull rotation channels (corrupt channels excluded from the statistics): median 0.75°, 90th percentile 2.6°; 586 channels exceed 1°, 110 exceed 3°. [v] NAF computed with GeckoLib's formula

**Keyframe-reduction estimate**

Method: per channel, greedily drop interior keys while the curve rebuilt from the kept keys (linear or catmull, as the channel uses, with GeckoLib 5's end-key rule) stays within tolerance of the original at every original key time and midpoint. Molang channels and corrupt values are kept as they are.

| Tolerance | Keys kept | Removed | Catmull channels removed | Linear channels removed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1° rotation, 0.01 units position and scale | 66,193 of 110,620 | 40.2% | 45.7% | 34.4% |
| 0.5° rotation, 0.05 units position and scale | 43,714 of 110,620 | 60.5% | n/a | n/a |

Tyrannosaurus alone drops from 43,812 to 22,555 keys (49%) at the tight tolerance and to 13,094 (70%) at the loose one. Rest-pose channels (11,643 keys, 1,432 bone entries) are inside these totals and can be removed exactly. Minifying the JSON alone takes 11.9 MB to 6.9 MB, but jar compression hides most of that. [v] NAF; [inf] for any visual claim.

### Inferences

- [inf] 4.x per-frame cost for one Tyrannosaurus playing `roar` (6,708 keys over 7.54 s): about three axes times half of 6,708, roughly 10,000 loop steps per controller per frame, plus about 900 queue objects for its 91 bones per controller. Halving the keys halves the scan. A held pose (sit, rest) costs the full list every frame. On 5.x the same animation costs about 90 bones times 9 axes, around 800 short checks, at any density.
- [inf] So on 4.x, reduction gives a real CPU win for herds of large animals; on 5.x, its main win is memory, load time and file size. Removing rest-pose bone entries helps both versions, because each listed bone costs nine evaluations and, on 5.x, a snapshot.
- [inf] Memory on 4.x is roughly 3 axes times (Keyframe record plus value objects), about 250-300 bytes per JSON key, so about 30 MB for 110,620 keys across six species, before the transient Gson tree. A 40-60% cut saves 12-18 MB. 5.x deduplicates constants and should use less. Not measured.
- [inf] Sparse keys with easing give the same visual result as dense baked keys when the tolerance is under about 0.5°: an error that small is hard to see at normal view distance, and 25 fps samples were themselves a linear approximation of the source curve. Re-authoring a baked clip by hand with 4-8 keys per channel and catmull or ease-in-out usually looks better, because it removes 25 Hz stair-stepping in fast motion. It costs artist time.
- [inf] The rest-pose channels look like an export of "all bones" from a rig or a template. On 4.x they are mostly harmless for transforms the other controller does not key, but on 5.x they make the main controller own every listed bone fully, so a rest-pose leg entry in an idle would freeze the walk legs if the idle ever plays with the walk.
- [inf] The corrupt values make a bone spin by millions of degrees across one segment, so it shows as a flicker or random pose for that frame range. They look like tiny numbers whose negative exponent was lost; replacing them with 0 is the likely fix, checked in Blockbench.
- [inf] How to undo baking in Blockbench: there is no built-in reducer, so the practical path is an offline script (the same greedy method as above) that rewrites the `.animation.json`, then a re-import into the `.bbmodel` with the GeckoLib plugin, or a one-time manual clean of the worst clips. Keep "Bake in bezier keyframes" off only if the runtime plays bezier (AzureLib, or a custom easing registered in GeckoLib); otherwise turn it on, or avoid bezier keys, because unbaked bezier plays linear.

### Gaps

- Load time and heap were not measured in game; the memory figure is an estimate.
- Which Blockbench tool baked each Nublar clip is not recorded in the files; the 24 and 25 per second spacing fits IK export, "Bake IK", bezier bake or Bakery.
- The reduction was checked numerically, not by eye in game.
- The plugin `optimize.js` and the Animated Java plugin were not read.

## Key question 4: Known pitfalls

### Takeaway

The 4.x traps Nublar meets are: stdout spam for missing animations and missing keyframe handlers, a playhead jump when speed changes, keyframe markers that fire early by the transition length, held animations that never "finish", and fresh controllers after every chunk reload. 5.x fixes most of them but changes behaviour Nublar depends on: no `forceAnimationReset`, hold now reports finished, a controller with zero transition snaps on stop, and a bone listed in an animation is owned fully.

### Cited Findings

- Missing animation (4.x): stdout line every frame, because the controller stops and retries on the next frame. [v] [GL4C AnimationProcessor L48-52](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/core/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/core/animation/AnimationProcessor.java#L48-L52); [GL4C AnimationController L342-367](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/core/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/core/animation/AnimationController.java#L342-L367). Nublar already guards this. [v] `common/src/main/java/net/dumbcode/projectnublar/util/DinoAnimationAvailability.java` L19-25
- Missing animation (5.x): silent for a missing name; an ERROR per lookup for a missing file. [v] [GL5 cache/BakedAnimationCache.java L27-57](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/cache/BakedAnimationCache.java#L27-L57)
- `forceAnimationReset` (4.x): marks the animation for reload, so the next `setAnimation` rebuilds the queue and enters TRANSITIONING; the restart blends over the transition length from the current pose, not a hard cut. [v] [GL4C AnimationController L276-278, L342-367](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/core/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/core/animation/AnimationController.java#L276-L278). [v] [GL5 AnimationController L276-290, L361-369](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animation/AnimationController.java#L276-L369)

[v] GL4C AnimationProcessor L131-209; [GL5 AnimationController L456-459](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animation/AnimationController.java#L456-L459); GL5 AnimationTimeline L199-213
- 5.x first animation with no previous point (fresh controller, or after `reset()`): the leading transition stage takes the "reset" path, which blends from the animation's first frame towards rest, then the animation starts at its first frame. [v] [GL5 AnimationProcessor L165-171, L222-235](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animation/AnimationProcessor.java#L222-L235); [GL5 AnimationController L287](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animation/AnimationController.java#L287)

[v] [GL4F GeoEntity L58-67](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/Fabric/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/animatable/GeoEntity.java#L58-L67); [GL5 AnimatableManager L30-36](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/1e09655d3b54c2db959d234266fb564b90aa9382/common/src/main/java/com/geckolib/animatable/manager/AnimatableManager.java#L30-L36)
- Animation tick vs partial tick (4.x): entity time is `tickCount + partialTick`; the model keeps one running clock shared by all entities of that model, advanced by the difference from the last rendered entity's time; nothing advances while the game is paused unless `shouldPlayAnimsWhileGamePaused`. [v] [GL4F GeoModel L189-212](https://github.com/bernie-g/geckolib/blob/7d6f32db9168a24e14b327c0ad9f8f87dfc204de/Fabric/src/main/java/software/bernie/geckolib/model/GeoModel.java#L189-L212)

- `ground_speed` comes from `getDeltaMovement()`. Nublar reads position change instead for its gait. [v] GL4F GeoModel L244-248; `common/src/main/java/net/dumbcode/projectnublar/entity/dinosaur/animation/DinoAnimator.java` L84-88

### Inferences

- [inf] Early markers: Nublar's main controller uses 5-tick transitions, so on 4.x a footstep or roar sound keyed in the file fires 0.25 s before the matching pose.
- [inf] For Nublar on 5.x, map `forceAnimationReset` to `reset()` followed by `setAnimation`, or to `setAnimationTime(0)` when the same animation must restart in place. Check the "fresh point" path above, because a restart after `reset()` may drift to rest and snap.
- [inf] Idle repick on 5.x: Nublar asks `hasAnimationFinished` only for looping idles today; held sit and rest would start reporting finished on 5.x, so any future logic that reads it for held states changes meaning.
- [inf] Chunk reload makes every animal in a loaded herd start its idle and walk at frame 0 together. A random start offset per entity (5.x `setAnimationTime`, 4.x a controller subclass that shifts its protected tick offset, AzureLib `SetStartTickOffset`) removes the marching-in-step look.
- [inf] Long or state-like actions (sleep, sit, death) should stay driven by synced entity data, as Nublar does now; triggers suit short one-shots only.
- [inf] The secondary controller with 0 ticks and constant STOP costs a controller pass per frame for nothing; remove it or give it a job.

### Gaps

- The 5.x "fresh point drifts to rest" path was read, not run.
- No test of what 4.x does with a `pre`-only key at time 0.
